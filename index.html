A new beginning

Architecture is no longer just about buildings

1999

  • A much narrower field
  • Fewer paths
  • Limited opportunities

Today

  • Sustainability, human experience, social responsibility
  • Smart, technology-driven, data-informed
  • Technology, environment and social sciences together
  • Holistic, adaptive, future-ready environments

You are stepping into the world at a very interesting time.

When I graduated in 1999, architecture was a much narrower field. The paths were fewer, the opportunities were limited.

Today, it has transformed.

Architecture is no longer just about buildings.

It is now creating meaningful impact through sustainability, human experience, and social responsibility. It has evolved from static, standalone design to smart, technology-driven, and data-informed solutions. The profession is now highly interdisciplinary, integrating insights from technology, environment, and social sciences. Overall, architecture has moved from simply constructing buildings to shaping holistic, adaptive, and future-ready environments.

And that makes this an incredibly exciting time to begin.
But at the same time… it can also feel overwhelming.

Because this moment is not just about finishing something.
It is about beginning something new… especially without a clear map.

And if you are feeling uncertain right now — that is completely okay.
In fact, it is a very honest place to be.

Today, I want to share three important experiences with you.

Not as instructions.
But as reflections from my own journey — things I understood slowly, sometimes the hard way.

First experience

Designing your path forward

1–2 of 10

In India, only about 1 to 2 out of 10 graduates actually continue in the exact field they studied.

Over the last five years, you have learned how to design.
But more importantly, you have learned how to think.

How to observe.
How to question.
How to approach problems with both logic and empathy.

And that is something that will stay with you — no matter where you go.

When I chose architecture, my decision was quite simple.
I heard it involved art and design, and the career later is all colorful… and that was enough for me.
I followed that instinct without really thinking too far ahead.
And honestly, I had a wonderful time.

But as I moved closer to graduation, I began to notice something that unsettled me.
Some of the seniors I admired most… were moving away from architecture.

And that created doubt.
It made me wonder — then what is the right path?

And I know many of you might be feeling something similar today.
That quiet question in your mind:

“Am I doing the right thing?”

Let me tell you something that took me time to understand—
You don’t have to have everything figured out right now.
And more importantly…

It is never too late to change direction.

Because what architecture has given you is not just a profession.
It has given you a way of thinking.
And that has value everywhere.

And here’s something worth reflecting on—
In India, only about 1 to 2 out of 10 graduates actually continue in the exact field they studied.

Most people evolve.
They shift.
They discover something new about themselves along the way.

So if you are uncertain today…
You are not lost.

You are just at the beginning of discovery.

I remember something very clearly from my fourth year.
That day, on the sofa in my living room, I was watching television, and I saw the Sony TV Entertainment channel identity.
And for some reason, I was completely drawn to it.
It felt different.
It felt alive.

And for the first time, I found myself thinking—
“What if I could do something like this?”

And almost immediately, I dismissed it.
I told myself — this is not your field.
You don’t know the tools, the technology, the process.
So I let that thought go.

But looking back…
That curiosity stayed.
And I’ve realized something since then—
The things that genuinely excite you…
They don’t come without a reason.

So don’t ignore them too quickly.

That curiosity eventually led me to make a decision. A decision for a change in my career.
That brings to my second topic of gyan bhodhan on how to Handle Change.

After architecture, and after working for about a year, I chose to explore motion design further.
I went on to pursue my Master’s in Fine Arts at the Savannah College of Art & Design (SCAD) in the United States.

Those were intense years.
I worked hard. I pushed myself.
And I graduated with three gold medals.

At that point, I felt ready.
I thought — now things will fall into place.

But before I go further, let me pause here and ask you something.
If you really think about it—
What is the percentage of the overall knowledge that comes from your college education?

College education: the foundation

Now when you hear this…
Some of you might think—
“Then why did I spend five years in college?”

But here’s how I look at it.
What you have learned here… is not small.
It is your foundation.
And in architecture, we all understand this better than anyone—

A building is only as strong as its foundation.

These five years of education.
They have built your way of thinking.
They have shaped your discipline.
They have trained your eye, your patience, your ability to solve problems.
That is not replaceable.

And now—
You are stepping onto the next level.
The next set of stepping stones in your journey.

Whether you choose higher studies… or step into the professional world…
Find places that help you build further on this foundation.
Choose environments that align with your purpose.
Go where your curiosity is challenged.
Go where your learning continues.
Not just where things are comfortable.

This is also a very unique phase of your life.
You have the freedom to experiment.
You have the space to take risks.
You don’t yet have the weight of long-term obligations.
So use this time wisely.

Reinforce your foundation.
Start shaping your career — not just strongly, but meaningfully.

And through all of this…
Trust your instincts.
Believe in your intuition.
Because more often than not…
That inner voice knows the direction… even when the path is not clear.

But life had a different plan.
In 2003, the US went through a major recession.
Opportunities became scarce.
For international students, it was even more challenging.
I applied to over 25 to 30 different companies.
Most of them in Los Angeles and Hollywood, which was the industry hub.
Surprisingly, the opportunity that came through…
Was in New York City.

I joined a design studio called Liquid Light Inc. in Manhattan.
And I still remember that moment.
I wasn’t hired as a fresh college graduate nor as a junior designer.
I was brought in as a Broadcast Designer. Two levels above for any recent college graduate.
It felt like everything had aligned.
Like this was the moment I had been working towards.

I began working closely with the Creative Director — Kieran Walsh.
And very quickly, I experienced something important.
I realized how much I still had to learn.
The way he thought, the way he approached design, the clarity he brought into every decision — it was humbling.
And at the same time, incredibly inspiring.
I felt like I was exactly where I needed to be.

And then…

11 months later… the company shut down.

No warning.
No preparation.
Just like that — everything changed.

And in that moment, I understood something I had never fully understood before—

We don’t grow when things go according to plan.
We grow when they don’t.

Defining success

What we call luck

Dashed line: the fox’s maximum capability.

That phase of my life slowly reshaped how I think about success.
Because success is not something you chase in a straight line.
It is something you discover… through movement.

Let me share a simple story.
The story of the fox and the grapes.

A fox sees grapes hanging just out of reach.
It jumps.
It tries again.
And again.
It reaches its maximum capability…
And still cannot get them.
And eventually, it stops.
And tells itself — the grapes are probably not worth it.

Now imagine this—
The fox is you.
The grapes are opportunity.
And the jumps… are your efforts.

Now imagine something else.
A small breeze.
A wind.

If the fox had tried just one more time…
At the exact moment that breeze lowered the grapes slightly…
It would have reached them.

That breeze…
Is what we call luck.

Luck is not something that appears out of nowhere.

Luck meets you… only when you are still trying.

So my friends—

Keep jumping.

Because you never know which one effort… will connect you to something bigger.

And this is something I often relate to Virender Sehwag.
Sehwag did not wait for the perfect moment.
He didn’t play safe.
Whether it was a Test match or a T20…
He played every ball with intent.
Confidence.
Freedom.

Did he fail sometimes?
Of course.
But that never stopped him from playing the next ball the same way.

And I think that is a powerful lesson.
Approach every opportunity fully.
Not because every opportunity will work.
But because that mindset keeps you alive in the game.

And yes…
You will make mistakes.
You will fail.

But here is something I strongly believe—

It is okay to make a mistake once.
But it is not okay to ignore what it was trying to teach you.

Growth is not about avoiding failure.
It is about becoming better because of it.

Stay open to feedback.
Learn continuously.
Build your skills not for display…
But for depth.

And most importantly—
Enjoy what you are doing.
Because this phase of your life…
Is not just about building a career.
It is about building yourself.

Closing

Success is external. Happiness is internal.

Success

External. What you achieve.

Happiness

Internal. How you feel while achieving it.

Before I conclude, I want to leave you with one final thought.

There is a difference between success and happiness.
Success is external.
Happiness is internal.
And the two don’t always go together.

That is why your priorities matter.
Because if your priorities are not aligned with what truly matters to you…
Even success can feel empty.

But when they are aligned…
Even small achievements feel meaningful.

And for me, that is what true success is—

Not just what you achieve.
But how you feel while achieving it.

So as you step into what comes next—
Take your time.
Stay open.
Stay curious.
Trust your journey.

Design your path with intention.

Embrace change with courage.

Define success in your own way.

Congratulations once again, Class of 2026.

I wish you strength in uncertainty, clarity in confusion, and joy in everything you choose to pursue.

Thank you… and all the very best.
